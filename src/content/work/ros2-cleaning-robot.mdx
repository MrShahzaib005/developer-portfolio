## 02 / Problem & Simulation Constraints

Developing autonomous mobile robots (AMRs) directly on physical hardware can be slow and risky:
1. **Hardware Scarcity:** Access to physical testing platforms with calibrated LiDARs and wheel encoders is constrained.
2. **Crash Risk During Tuning:** Untuned navigation costmaps can drive a robot into physical furniture, damaging motors or chassis parts.
3. **Reproducibility:** Testing whether a robot can clean 90%+ of a room requires identical initial states, which is impossible in a changing physical office without ground-truth motion tracking.

```
[START] Gazebo world initializes with furniture obstacles and walls.
   │
   ▼

   │
   ▼
[PHASE 2: MAPPING]
SLAM node converts scan beams and wheel ticks into a 2D black-and-white map.
Black = Walls/Obstacles, White = Cleanable Floor, Gray = Unexplored Space.
   │
   ▼
[PHASE 3: PATH EXECUTION]
Navigation engine plots a boustrophedon (lawnmower) path across white space.
Motors steer around tables and recover automatically if trapped in corners.
```

## 05 / Key Technical Decisions & Trade-offs

### 1. Costmap Inflation Layer Tuning
- **Trade-off:** A large inflation radius keeps the robot safely away from walls but prevents it from cleaning tight corridors and door thresholds. A small inflation radius causes wheels to clip obstacle borders.
- **Decision:** Settled on an inflation radius of 0.22m with an exponential cost decay factor of 3.0, allowing the robot to navigate doorways while ensuring a 5cm physical clearance buffer.

### 2. SLAM Toolbox vs. Cartographer
- **Decision:** Chose SLAM Toolbox in asynchronous mode for its superior memory efficiency in mid-size simulated office floor plans and robust map serialization.
